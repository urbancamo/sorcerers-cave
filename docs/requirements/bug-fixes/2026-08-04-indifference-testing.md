# Indifference Testing

There is a rules gap for indifference testing that needs to be filled. It requires more detailed explanation.

Here are some issues:
 - If I withdraw into a chamber that has strangers in that have tested indifferent, I don't get the option to re-test them. I think this is mandatory. Only after the testing should I be able to  
   continue again. 
 - Unless they are permanently indifferent. In this case you should indicate that they are permanently indifferent, give the option to attack, but no retest and the option to move
 - If the party tests the strangers and the result is indifferent, and they subsequently attempt to move and a dead-end is drawn, then they must retest again unless permanently indifferent 

I think it will be worth defining the rules for indifference testing in a separate document so that all scenarios are
captured. This is the first requirement, before implementing these changes.

NOTE: in the initial implementation, ignore the NO WAY OUT rule below.

## Peter's Notes
Here are Peter's notes, providing some clarification of the rules:

"If the strangers are indifferent (*), in its next turn the party may test them again, or attack them, or leave the chamber by any doorway without picking up any treasure found there.
If the party chooses to remain in the chamber, or finds itself delayed by a dead end, or if at any time it re-enters the chamber, then it must in the same turn either test the strangers again or attack them. Meanwhile other parties entering the chamber have the usual options."

So I'm not quite sure if I'm understanding you correctly.

In Solitaire Play:

You can certainly leave on your next turn by any direction that you definitely know is open. When you draw a dead end trying to leave, or if you choose to remain, or if you go away and later re-enter, then you must test again or attack. They remember forever how many times your party has approached them - even if you went away in between, and even (curiously?) if your party now has no creatures in common with the party they remember approaching them previously.

So "indifference" the first 2 times is a veiled threat: "We don't mind you as long as you are just passing through...". In Solitaire play, after the 3rd indifferent result, it is "We remember you and we've decided you don't matter..."

If the way you arrived is blocked (say, Earthquake or Spell), and there are 3 unexplored exits in this chamber, it is then it is possible to end up being required to test the Strangers 3 times - for example:

 - Turn 1 = Enter a chamber from N direction. 4 doorways N/E/W/S. Draw Earthquake and some Strangers. Approach = Indifferent.
 - Turn 2 = Try to leave by E doorway = Dead End. Approach again = Indifferent.
 - Turn 3 = Try to leave by W doorway = Dead End. Approach again = Indifferent (In Solitaire Play, they are indifferent for the rest of the game).
 - Turn 4 = Leave by S doorway - which can't be a Dead End because of the "no way out" rule:
"Occasionally, it may happen that a player or players meet nothing but dead ends wherever they turn, and cannot continue exploring. If all available doorways and stairways have been tried, the last area card played to make a dead end may in the same turn be put back into the middle of the pack, and another one drawn, until a way is found. This course cannot be followed when there is any other means of continuing the exploration, however time consuming, difficult, or dangerous."

With respect to the "no way out" rule, I interpret "any other means of continuing" as being very broad - it covers all methods of travelling. If your only way of continuing is to use your Magic Carpet, then you don't get to redraw the last area - you must use the artefact next turn.
