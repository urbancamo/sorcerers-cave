Precise Locations — Implementation Plan

Giving every party token — and every dropped treasure — an exact spot on the tile: a doorway, the chamber's centre, or a special-area's island.

1 Goals & scope

Today, a party's location is just "the area" — rendered at the centre of the tile, whichever doorway it actually walked in through. This plan gives every party (and every dropped item) a precise sub-tile position: which doorway it's standing in, whether it has settled into the chamber's centre, or — in the Viper Pit, Deep Pool, Whirlpool and Chasm — whether it's on the central island.

  • Every chamber and tunnel gets a doorway-vs-centre distinction, not just the four special areas — this is Peter's original, broader house rule (see §2).
  • The four special areas (Viper Pit, Deep Pool, Whirlpool, Chasm) get a doorway-vs-island distinction, matching the rulebook's own "stop at the edge, cross next turn" language and Peter's Case 1/2/3 walkthroughs.
  • Dropped treasure gets the same precision: a Deep Pool drop sits in the doorway (or on the island) it was left in, colour-coded to the dropping party, not lumped into one tile-wide pile.
  • This is a design-review document, not code. Per the brief, no code changes ship from this plan alone — it exists so the designer can confirm the model below matches intent before implementation starts.
Sources This plan synthesises three documents: the brief (docs/requirements/precise-locations/2027-07-29-precise-locations-requirements.md), Peter's detailed walkthrough (precise-locations-peters-notes.md, same folder — Viper Pit/Deep Pool/Whirlpool/Chasm Cases 1–3, plus the dropped-treasure section and mock-ups), and his earlier, broader note on chambers & doorways in docs/requirements/multiplayer/sorcerers-cave-notes-from-peter.md:105-131. It also reconciles with two places this idea was already sketched but never built: the multiplayer plan's §12 item ③ "doorway/scout vs entered sub-state" and multiplayer-interaction-specification.md's I-14 ("a party stands on the island/doorway per its own crossing state").

2 What already exists today

Good news first: the hard part of the underlying rule — "you stop when you first enter, and only cross on a later turn" — is already implemented at the engine level. This plan is mostly a presentation and data-precision layer on top of a sound foundation, plus a handful of genuine rules decisions called out in §8.

Already correct — the two-turn gate SC-10-3: "Entry stops at the edge: on the entry turn the party lands on a Pit/Pool tile and the turn ends in explore — no crossing yet." SC-10-4: crossing fires only when partyArea !== oldPrev — i.e. leaving by a different doorway than you arrived by, never on retracing. (reduce.ts:598-634, docs/specs/engine-spec.md:309-310) Because every move consumes a turn, this structurally already forces "enter this turn, decide next turn" — we don't need to invent that gate, only make it visible.

2.1 The compass is already a first-class concept

Directions are not an abstract adjacency graph — every area card decodes to explicit n/e/s/w/stairUp/stairDown exit bits (packages/engine/src/decode.ts:1-28), and DIR_N=1, DIR_E=2, DIR_S=3, DIR_W=4, DIR_UP=5, DIR_DOWN=6 (coords.ts:1-6) resolve to real grid coordinates via targetCoord (coords.ts:19-29), since every PlacedArea.coord is a packed level·10000 + y·100 + x (state.ts:62). This means the compass direction a party arrived by is always geometrically recoverable — compare the current area's coordinate to state.prev's — even though nothing stores it explicitly today (see §5).

2.2 The renderer already has the geometry for doorways

The 3D view (apps/web/src/view/cave3d.js) already computes exactly the offsets we need, just for a different purpose — exit chevrons:

// cave3d.js:288 — the North/South/East/West edge offsets from tile-centre
const off={N:[0,0,-(TILE_D/2+edge)],S:[0,0,TILE_D/2+edge],E:[TILE_W/2+edge,0,0],W:[-(TILE_W/2+edge),0,0]}[m.dir];

Two more precedents already exist for "more than one thing on a tile": the secret-door card marker sits at a fixed tile corner (cave3d.js:220), and multiplayer already fans overlapping party tokens in a small ring around the centre so they don't overlap (cave3d.js:759-761). Treasure/creature/hazard cards are already laid in fixed lanes offset from tile-centre, not stacked dead-centre (cave3d.js:429-433). None of these are doorway-aware yet, but they are the exact mechanism this plan reuses.

What's missing No code anywhere models a sub-tile position: no arrivedDir/entryDir field, no "island" concept, and the party token is always placed at exactly worldPos(area) — tile-centre, full stop (cave3d.js:19,236). Dropped treasure is a single flat list per tile (PlacedArea.contents, PlacedArea.dropped — state.ts:65,68), never tagged with a sub-location. docs/specs/engine-spec.md Appendix B says so explicitly: "Deep-Pool drops all heavy treasure to a single dropped list on the tile (no per-doorway model)"; the Milestone C-3 plan names the same simplification on delivery: "stair/trap arrival 'on the island' is not specially modelled" (docs/requirements/2026-06-12-milestone-c3-special-areas-plan.md:355).

3 The sub-location model

Three positions cover every case in Peter's notes. This is the vocabulary the rest of the plan uses:

Doorway. The default on arrival: party token sits at the edge it walked in through. Withdraw is live; the party is "scouting."

Centre. Once settled — small cards drawn, traps/hazards survived, and the player has committed (Approach/Attack/Loot) rather than merely tested or withdrawn.

Island. Special areas only (Viper Pit, Deep Pool, Chasm) — reached via a secret Up/Down stair, or a trap fall. Whirlpool has no island (§4).

3.1 For every ordinary chamber and tunnel

Peter's broader house rule, from his original note: "I always play a house rule for new areas whereby I place my party token initially in the entrance doorway (for all chambers, not just e.g. Viper Pit etc.)... this is where they 'scout' the chamber, draw small cards, encounter traps and hazards, and wait... versus the party token being moved into, across or around the centre of the chamber, approaching or surprise attacking strangers, looting, traversing." His follow-up note is the precise trigger list: the token stays in the doorway until any of "drawn small cards," "survived traps & hazards," or "been presented with the Withdraw/Approach/Attack options" — i.e. it moves to centre only once the player acts on one of those options, not merely because they were offered.

3.2 For the four special areas

Doorway vs. island, gated by how the party arrived — laterally (N/E/S/W) vs. vertically (a secret stair or a trap fall). Whirlpool is the outlier: Peter's rules make it structurally island-less (see §4).

4 The four special chambers, case by case

Peter's notes give each area distinct connectivity rules once you're inside — these are not the same shape, and the diagrams below (redrawn from his three Viper Pit mock-ups, viper-pit-case-1/2/3.png) make the differences explicit for sign-off.

Viper Pit. From a doorway: the two adjacent doorways only (N and S from a W entry), around the ledge, one d6/creature (brass curves) — never straight across to E (dashed red). From the island: any of the four, jump-risk d6/creature (teal spokes, house rule). special.ts:15-37.

Deep Pool. From a doorway: any of the other three (brass), gated only by Giant/heavy-treasure carrying capacity — no adjacency limit, no fatal roll. From the island: same, any doorway. special.ts:39-56.

Whirlpool. Same adjacent-only shallows as the Pit (N and S from a W entry, never straight across to E), but one shared roll for the whole party — and no island at all: a stairway here is blocked outright, a trap sends you a level further down. special.ts:58-68.

Chasm. No lateral crossing at all — only retrace, descend, or (island only) an already-discovered secret door. Descent is the only real "crossing." reduce.ts:1258-1271.

5 How the engine computes it

The reassuring finding: for the common cases, sub-location needs no new persisted state — it's a pure function of fields that already exist. Only dropped-treasure precision and the "adjacency"/"jump" rules (§8) need real engine changes.

5.1 Doorway direction — geometry, not a new field

Since every PlacedArea.coord is an absolute packed grid coordinate (state.ts:62), the compass direction of arrival is recoverable by comparing the current area's (x,y,level) to state.prev's via unpackCoord (coords.ts:12-16): same level, y−1 → arrived from the south (standing in the North doorway of a fresh tile is wrong framing — restated: the doorway a party stands in is the one whose compass direction points back at prev). A level change with the same (x,y) is a vertical arrival (secret stair or trap) → island. This is a pure, stateless helper — no reducer change, no new field, callable from the renderer alone.

5.2 Doorway vs. centre — already encoded in phase

This is the pleasant surprise. GamePhase is "explore" | "medusa" | "encounter" | "fight" | "pickup" | "gameOver" (state.ts:28). phase is set to "encounter" the moment a fresh chamber is drawn (reduce.ts:325), and withdraw/test/attack are only legal while phase === "encounter" (reduce.ts:641,841,908) — i.e. exactly the window Peter describes as "scouting from the doorway." The instant the party commits — a won/skipped fight moves to "explore" or a loot session opens "pickup" — is exactly the moment his notes say the token moves to centre. So: phase === "encounter" ⇒ doorway; otherwise ⇒ centre. No new field.

5.3 Island vs. doorway for the specials — mostly free, one caveat

Combine §5.1 (vertical vs. lateral arrival) with the existing fellThroughTrap flag (state.ts:137, already distinguishes a trap landing from a secret-stair landing for the withdraw-legality check) to tell a stair-island from a trap-island apart where the distinction matters for legal actions — it never matters for position, since both land you on the island (confirmed by Peter's Case 2 vs. Case 3, which differ only in whether "go back" is offered, not in where you stand).

The one real gap None of this tells the renderer which doorway a party is in once it has taken further in-place actions without moving (e.g. a multi-turn Viper Pit ledge-walk, §4) — today's state has no "current sub-location" field, only "last area moved from." Recommendation: add one small, explicit field rather than lean on fragile geometry for the special-area ledge-walk: GameState.subLocation?: { area: number; at: "doorway" | "island"; dir?: 1|2|3|4 } (mirrored per-seat on PartyState for multiplayer, §9), set by tryMove/resolveAreaLoop alongside the existing prev/ prev2 bookkeeping. Ordinary chambers don't need it (phase already covers them, §5.2); it's scoped to the four specials only.

6 Rendering plan

Reuses existing geometry and precedents from §2.2 — no new visual language, just doorway-aware placement of things that are currently always tile-centred.

Moving between sub-locations on the same tile (e.g. doorway → centre once settled, or a Viper Pit ledge-walk) is a same-tile lerp, exactly like the existing tokenMove animation used for full tile-to-tile moves (cave3d.js, doMove/refresh) — just a shorter hop.

6.2 Other parties (multiplayer)

OtherPartyToken (CaveCanvas.tsx:14) is {color, col, row, level} — no sub-location field. Extend it with the same {at, dir} shape as §5.3, and replace the generic radial fan in setOtherParties (cave3d.js:759-761, currently just "which arrival order are you, fan by 120°") with the doorway offset from §6.1 — this is a strict upgrade: two rival parties in different doorways of the same Deep Pool now render exactly where they are, rather than jittered around the centre regardless of truth.

6.3 New geometry: the island

Today buildAreaMesh renders every tile identically — a flat textured plane, no per-special geometry (cave3d.js:96-111). A believable island needs a small raised disc/rock mesh at tile-centre for Viper Pit/Deep Pool/Chasm tiles only (checked via decodeArea(card).special, already available). This is the one place this plan asks for new 3D art/geometry rather than pure repositioning — scope it modestly (a simple raised cylinder + rim, not bespoke sculpted art) unless the designer wants more.

7 Precise dropped treasure

Peter's own example, verbatim: "The party stops in the East doorway of the Deep Pool. Next turn, they drop heavy treasure and swim to the South doorway. The dropped treasure is represented by a Dropped Item Token... Now the party have 3 chances to find an open route."

Heavy treasure left in the East doorway (red marker, colour-coded to the party in play) while the party swims on to the South doorway — exactly Peter's walkthrough.

7.1 Data model change (this one is real)

Today PlacedArea.dropped?: number[] is one flat list per tile, written by deepPoolCrossing (special.ts:41-56) and read back by resolveAreaLoop's Giant-only reclaim (reduce.ts:381-390) — no sub-location tag. Proposed shape:

// PlacedArea, state.ts:68 — was: dropped?: number[]
dropped?: { at: "island" | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4; owner?: PartyColor; items: number[] }[];
// 1..4 = DIR_N..DIR_W (coords.ts); owner is solo-optional, required in multiplayer (§9)

Every read site (the Giant-reclaim check, the pool crossing that pushes to it) narrows to "the bucket matching the party's current sub-location" rather than the whole tile — matching the rulebook language for both areas ("recoverable only by a giant" says nothing about location; Peter's notes are the ones that make it precise).

Depends on §8 This data model is only worth building once "voluntarily cast treasure into the pit/pool" actually sinks it (see §8.3) — today dropTreasure never special-cases these tiles at all, so a designer sign-off on where a drop lands is moot until sign-off on whether it's protected.

7.2 Scope: Deep Pool first, or all four?

The brief calls Deep Pool "the most obvious" case; Peter's notes (§DROPPED TREASURE/ARTEFACTS) argue for all four — Viper Pit becomes "your very own safe deposit box" with the Charmed Flute, and precise Chasm/Whirlpool drops support the Eye-of-God-hiding strategies he describes. This is flagged as a scope decision in §11, not assumed.

8 Rules gaps this work surfaces

Building precise sub-locations forces four latent rules questions into the open — they were invisible while position was purely cosmetic (tile-centre for everyone), but a designer sign-off on where a party stands has to also cover what it can do from there.

8.1 Adjacency-gated crossing (Viper Pit & Whirlpool)

openMoves() (apps/web/src/view/engineAdapter.ts:64-79) offers every compass exit the card has, unconditionally, via legalActions (packages/engine/src/selectors.ts:105) — there is no concept of "reachable from your current sub-location." Implementing §4's "adjacent doorways only" for the Pit and Whirlpool is a real legality change, not just a rendering one: openMoves/legalActions need to consult subLocation (§5.3) and mask out the non-adjacent doorway(s).

8.2 "Jump to the island" is an undocumented house rule

Peter is explicit that this is his addition, not the printed rules: "I play the jumping/swimming house rule for Viper Pit and Deep Pool, even though the rules do not specify that you can... without the jumping action... falling down a trap onto the central island would be an unavoidable Total Party Kill." No GameAction for this exists in the current 22-action catalog (actions.ts:4-42, SC-4-41). Building it means a new action (e.g. jumpToIsland), legal only from a lateral doorway of a Pit/Pool tile, with Viper Pit rolling a die per creature (fatal on the same 1–2, mirroring viperCrossing) and Deep Pool gating on Giant/heavy-treasure capacity exactly like its ordinary crossing.

8.3 Deliberately casting treasure into the Pit/Pool doesn't sink it today

Found while researching this plan dropTreasure (reduce.ts:798-818) always lands an item as ordinary floor contents (200+tid) or the live pickup set — it has no special case for standing on a Deep Pool or Viper Pit tile. So "treasure may be deliberately cast into the pool[/pit], and is recoverable only by a giant[/the Charmed Flute]" (rulebook, both areas) is not modelled: a deliberate drop today is ordinary, unprotected, anyone-can-loot-it treasure. This is exactly the mechanic Peter's "safe deposit box" strategy (§DROPPED TREASURE/ARTEFACTS, point 1) depends on, and it needs fixing before §7's per-doorway precision is worth building — otherwise we'd be precisely rendering a mechanic that doesn't protect anything.

8.4 The Whirlpool's "stairway blocked" rule isn't enforced

Peter's notes: "Any stairway leading to this area is considered blocked." Nothing in map.ts's stair-mirroring (mirrorReturnStair, map.ts:56-64) or reduce.ts's Whirlpool branch (403-408, 612-634) special-cases SPECIAL_WHIRLPOOL — a secret stair could in principle land a party there today, which this plan's design assumes never happens (§4 draws no island for the Whirlpool). Low-severity — the base card set may never produce this configuration in practice — but worth an explicit ruling since this plan's "Whirlpool has no island" claim depends on it never happening.

9 Multiplayer considerations

Everything above is written engine-first, matching Peter's own framing of the doorway/scout idea as "engine-level, so solitaire benefits too" (multiplayer plan §12 ③) — but two multiplayer-specific needs are worth flagging now rather than retrofitting later.

  • Per-seat sub-location. The sketched MpGameState/PartyState shape (multiplayer plan §5.2) already carries partyArea/prev/prev2 per party, so §5's derivation and §5.3's new subLocation field apply per-PartyState unchanged — no new concept, just N copies of the same one.
  • Independent crossings, already specified. multiplayer-interaction-specification.md I-14 already states the target behaviour precisely: "each party crosses independently; a party stands on the island/doorway per its own crossing state... PvP is illegal while either party is in the pool[/pit]." This plan is the missing engine/render layer under that already-agreed spec row — no new design decision needed here, just cross-referencing it as the multiplayer acceptance criterion.
  • Ownership on dropped markers is mandatory, not optional. The brief itself flags this: solo play never needs to know whose drop a marker is, but "in multiplayer, the tokens would have to be identifiable in case there were more than one pile of dropped stuff" — hence owner on the per-doorway bucket in §7.1's data model is required once variants/multiplayer is live, using the existing PARTY_COLOR_HEX palette (apps/web/src/game/partyColors.ts:8-13) for the marker tint — the same colours already used for tokens and secret-door washes.

10 Phased roadmap

Phase 1 — Doorway vs. centre, ordinary chambers presentation only no engine change
Implement §5.1 (geometry-derived direction) and §5.2 (phase-derived doorway/centre) as a pure renderer helper; offset the local party token per §6.1. Zero engine/data changes — the lowest-risk, most broadly visible slice, and matches Peter's simplest ask ("for all chambers, not just Viper Pit").
Phase 2 — Island positioning for the four specials small engine change
Add subLocation (§5.3) to GameState/PartyState; render the island for Viper Pit/Deep Pool/Chasm (§6.3); Whirlpool stays doorway-only pending §8.4's ruling.
Phase 3 — Fix the underlying rules gaps real rules work
§8.3 (deliberate cast-into-pit/pool actually sinks the item) is a prerequisite for Phase 4, not optional polish. §8.1 (adjacency-gated crossing) and §8.2 (jump-to-island action) are bundled here since they share the same legalActions/openMoves surface.
Phase 4 — Precise dropped treasure
The per-doorway dropped shape (§7.1), scoped per §11's decision on Deep-Pool-only vs. all four; colour-coded markers reusing PARTY_COLOR_HEX.
Phase 5 — Multiplayer wiring
Extend OtherPartyToken with sub-location (§6.2); mandatory owner on dropped markers (§9); confirm against multiplayer-interaction-specification.md I-14's already-agreed acceptance criteria.

11 Questions for the designer

  • Scope of precise dropped treasure (§7.2). Deep Pool only (the brief's "most obvious" case), or all four specials as Peter's fuller notes argue for (the safe-deposit-box and Eye-hiding strategies depend on Viper Pit/Whirlpool/Chasm too)?
  • Jump-to-island (§8.2). Confirm adopting Peter's explicit house rule (otherwise a trap fall onto the Viper Pit island with no Magic Carpet is an unavoidable Total Party Kill, which he argues can't have been the designer's intent) — and confirm the proposed resolution (Viper Pit: per-creature d6, same fatal 1–2; Deep Pool: Giant/heavy-treasure gate, no roll).
  • Whirlpool stairway-block (§8.4). Should this plan also close that gap (stairs never mirror onto a Whirlpool tile), or is it low-enough risk to leave as a known, separate gap?
  • New geometry budget (§6.3). A modest raised-island mesh for three special tiles, or is bespoke art wanted for this — and if so, is that this plan's scope or a separate art pass?
  • Sequencing (§10). Does Phase 1 (ordinary chambers, zero engine risk) ship alone as a visible early win, or does the whole plan land together?
Bottom line Most of this feature is cheap: doorway vs. centre for every ordinary chamber is a derived, stateless render change (Phase 1). The genuine new engine work is small and well-scoped (one subLocation field, one per-doorway treasure shape) — the real decisions the designer needs to make aren't about the engine at all, they're the four rules questions in §8 that this exercise surfaced.
