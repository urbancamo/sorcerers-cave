[1] Party outnumbers strangers (A).
The principle is not simply about removing redundant pauses, it is also about the game catering for
how human players operate. Although a computer can analyse a large number of possible
deployments very quickly, a human operating mostly by sight (albeit supplemented by mentally
visualising possible deployments to some extent) cannot easily manage many permutations. The
player needs to be able to “simulate” possible deployments by literally moving the cards into
tentative positions, evaluating, and swapping them around until the player believes they have
detected an optimal step (e) final deployment. Then the dice can be triggered.
This is a second general principle that sits alongside the “no redundant pauses” principle. In all
scenarios where the strangers have no choices, but the player has many choices spanning two steps,
for example (d) and (e), then the human player must be allowed to combine (d) and (e) into one card
placement session, with nothing finalised until the player confirms. The game only needs to check
that the final deployment chosen by the player as they hit the “Roll the Round” button is legal.
