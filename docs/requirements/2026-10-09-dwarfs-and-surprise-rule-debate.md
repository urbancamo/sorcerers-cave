# Dwarfs, Traps and the Advantage of Surprise: A Rules Debate

`DATE: 09-OCT-2026 (second version, after the question was reworded)`
`RESPONDS TO: [2026-10-09-dwarfs-and-surprise-rule-check.md](2026-10-09-dwarfs-and-surprise-rule-check.md)`
`SOURCE TEXT: [Expanded Consolidated Rules PV2026 V2.md](../rules/Expanded%20Consolidated%20Rules%20PV2026%20V2.md)`
`METHOD: reading of the rules text only; the code was not consulted`

## 1. The two questions

| # | Question | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Should a party with a dwarf be allowed to fall through a trap **deliberately**? | **Yes.** Otherwise a dwarf party is worse off than a party without one. |
| Q2 | Should the **first** fall through a trap carry no surprise, while **later** falls (deliberate or accidental) carry the +1? | **Not as an interpretation of the text.** The re-entry case it is built around is already covered by the existing wording. Only one part of the proposal changes anything, and that part has no textual support. See section 4. |

The proposal has three parts, referred to below as the first, second and third part:

- **First part.** The first party to fall through a trap has no surprise. The trap's warning system fires and alerts the strangers
  below.
- **Second part.** Later falls through the same trap, deliberate or accidental, **do** carry surprise, because the warning has
  already been spent.
- **Third part.** A party that has already visited the chamber below and withdrawn may return to attack, possibly through a trap
  that another party found, deliberately, with or without a dwarf.

## 2. The relevant wording, with the clues marked

**The trap card.** *"Your entire party goes to the area one level below. Any creatures or treasure found in this chamber
remain here. **Continues in effect.**"*

**The trap entry.** A party which falls down a trap ignores any treasure or creature cards found with it, but proceeds
normally with the turn in the area below. *"A party which includes a dwarf **may ignore** a trap, but not two traps drawn
in the same chamber. If a party **guided by** a dwarf becomes involved in a fight in a chamber where there is a trap, and
the dwarf is killed, the party falls down the trap as soon as it tries to leave the chamber."*

**The dwarf.** *"Guides past TRAPS."* Zombies *"will fall down traps unless accompanied by a living dwarf."*

**Surprise.** The side with surprise adds 1 to all its die rolls **in the first round**. A party gains it *"when
attacking immediately after coming into the chamber by a doorway or stairway **that has not been used before**, or when
arriving by magic carpet. **There is no advantage when the party has fallen down a trap, unless it has done so
deliberately.**"* Strangers gain it *"when they attack on being approached."*

**Between exploring parties.** Surprise comes *"only when attacking immediately after arriving in the area by **another
way than the one by which the defender entered**"*; *"you cannot gain the advantage over a party which you are following."*

**Other places that mention traps.**

- Crypt: the trap there *"has effect **once only** and then this card is discarded."*
- Well: *"A trap drawn here **continues to affect the whole area**."*
- Mutiny: the mutineers *"will not fall down a trap in the area."*
- A fall puts the party at *"the approximate centre of an area directly underneath"*, with markers on the two cards
  recording the link between them.

Four facts fall out of this text and frame everything below:

1. **Traps are not single-shot as written.** *"Continues in effect"* is the general rule, and the one single-shot trap (the
   Crypt) says so explicitly. If anything is single-shot, it is the proposed *warning*, not the trap.
2. **The surprise rule already separates deliberate falls from accidental ones.** It has one test for traps:
   deliberateness. It does not mention dwarfs, and it does not mention how many times the trap has been used.
3. **Surprise is tied to the route, and to novelty.** A doorway or stairway must be *"not used before"*, and the
   party-versus-party rule asks for *"another way"* from the one the defender knows. Both concern what the defenders
   can expect.
4. **A non-dwarf party cannot ignore a trap.** Entering a chamber where a trap is known to sit means falling. Whether
   that is "deliberate" depends on whether the party chose to enter. This matters for both questions.

## 3. Question 1: may a dwarf's party fall through a trap on purpose?

### 3.1 The case for allowing it

1. **There would be an anomaly otherwise.** A party with no dwarf that knows of a trap can walk into the chamber to use it
   (this is the user's own scenario: a trap found by another player's party, triggered deliberately, with or without
   a dwarf). That is a deliberate fall. A dwarf party, which is supposed to be *better* at dealing with traps, would
   be the only party unable to do so. A rule that makes the specialist worse off than the amateur is hard to attribute
   to the designers.
2. **"May ignore" is permissive.** *"May"* gives the player the power to ignore, not a compulsion. A power you may use is
   a power you may decline, and declining is going down.
3. **Nothing forbids it.** The dwarf clauses limit how much a dwarf can protect (*"not two traps"*), and what happens when
   the guide dies. None says a dwarf party is barred from using a trap. A rule that takes an option away ought to say so.
4. **The surprise sentence is written for all parties.** *"Unless it has done so deliberately"* says nothing about
   dwarfs. It applies to a dwarf party that elects to fall as much as to any other.

### 3.2 The case against

1. **"Ignore" means disregard.** A party that ignores a trap treats it as absent. The text gives the dwarf a way to avoid
   a fall and nowhere describes a dwarf *causing* one.
2. **"Guides past" describes an escort.** The dwarf is a passive protection, as it is for zombies. Nobody reads the zombie
   rule as letting zombies choose to fall.
3. **Deliberate falls do not need dwarfs.** Because other parties can already fall on purpose, the word *deliberately*
   cannot be used as proof that the designers meant dwarf parties to have the option.
4. **Where the designers wanted optional descent they said so.** The Chasm says *"you may go down a level."* Traps
   say no such thing for dwarf parties.

### 3.3 Weighing Q1

The "against" case is correct about the wording of *ignore* and *guides*, and it is right that the word *deliberately*
does not prove the point. But the "for" case does not depend on that word. It rests on the anomaly in 3.1.1 and on the
absence of any prohibition. Allowing the choice gives the dwarf party nothing that other parties lack.

**Verdict: allow it.** A party guided by a living dwarf may choose to fall through a trap instead of ignoring it, and
the fall is **deliberate** for the surprise rule. For two traps in one chamber, where the dwarf cannot ignore them, the
fall is not a choice.

## 4. Question 2: does the first fall lose surprise, and later falls gain it?

### 4.1 What the proposal changes

It helps to set the proposal beside the current wording, case by case.

| Case | Current text | Proposal |
|---|---|---|
| First fall, accidental (the trap is drawn) | No surprise | No surprise (no change) |
| First fall, **deliberate** (a dwarf party chooses to descend) | **Surprise** | **No surprise** (changed) |
| Later fall, deliberate (with or without a dwarf) | Surprise | Surprise (no change) |
| Later fall, accidental | No surprise | **Surprise** (changed) |

Two observations follow.

- **The third part needs no change to the rules.** A party that has visited the chamber below, withdrawn, and later returns on
  purpose by a trap found by another party is making a deliberate fall. Under the current text it already gets surprise,
  provided it attacks immediately. The reworded question hopes to give this result, and the text already does.
- **After the first use, a truly accidental fall is nearly impossible.** The trap card stays in the chamber and *"continues
  in effect"*, so anyone who sees the chamber knows what will happen. The remaining accidental cases are narrow: the
  guide-killed clause (*"the party falls down the trap as soon as it tries to leave"*), and a party that has forgotten
  or never saw the trap.

So the proposal's real content is two flips of explicit text: **removing surprise from the first deliberate fall** and **granting it
to the rare accidental later fall**.

### 4.2 The case for the proposal

1. **The warning idea is coherent.** If surprise means the strangers are unprepared, a physical trap with an alarm
   gives a first-time warning that a later fall would not repeat. It is a consistent story, and it unites the two
   rows that change.
2. **The third part is supported by the "another way" principle.** Between parties, surprise comes from arriving by a different way
   than the one the defender knows. A party that left the chamber below by stairs and comes back by a trap arrives
   by a way the occupants have not seen it use.
3. **Withdrawing does not spoil the return.** The party knows what is in the chamber; the strangers do not know when it
   will come back. A deliberate, prepared attack from an unexpected direction is the sort of thing surprise is meant
   to reward.

### 4.3 The case against the proposal

1. **The direction of the novelty test is reversed.** The text gives surprise to a route that has **not** been used
   before. The proposal gives surprise to a trap that **has** been used. If the rules' own logic is applied to traps, the first use
   would be the *most* surprising and later uses less so, the opposite of the proposal. It can only escape this by arguing that the
   trap is different because it carries an alarm, and the alarm is not in the text.
2. **The text already has a criterion for traps, and it is deliberateness.** The sentence on traps says
   *"unless it has done so deliberately"*. It does not mention order of use. The proposal overrides an explicit test with an
   unwritten one, which makes it an amendment, not a reading.
3. **The proposal contradicts the text twice.** It takes surprise away from a deliberate fall (the first one) and gives it to an
   accidental fall (a later one). Each reverses a clear statement.
4. **The rule's reason for denying surprise to falls is most naturally about the party, not the strangers.** A party that
   tumbles down a trap arrives disordered. A party that goes down on purpose is ready. That story explains the
   *deliberate/accidental* line exactly, and it applies equally to the first and the hundredth fall. The warning story
   explains nothing the text actually says.
5. **The trap "continues in effect".** The designers knew how to write a single-shot trap (the Crypt). The general trap
   is not one, so there is no "spent" alarm to appeal to.
6. **The warning has no one to warn in some cases.** The chamber below may be newly discovered, with its cards drawn only
   after arrival. Or the first party may have killed everyone there. And in the third part's own scenario the strangers have
   already met the party, so "warning" is the wrong frame: they are not unaware, and whether they are *unprepared* is not
   something the first fall decides.
7. **Multiplayer visibility does not help.** The trap being visible to every player affects what the *players* know. It
   says nothing about what the *strangers* expect, so it cannot carry the argument.

### 4.4 Weighing Q2

The two sides are not equally placed. The "for" case offers a plausible story and one useful principle ("another
way"), but the story is outside the text and the principle already works through the existing deliberateness test.
The "against" case points to explicit wording, and to a reversal of the rules' own novelty logic.

- **What is validated:** the third part. A party returning on purpose by a trap, with or without a dwarf, gets
  surprise, because it has fallen *deliberately*. No change to the rules is needed, and it is worth saying so in the
  rules.
- **What is not validated:** the first part for deliberate falls, and the second part for accidental ones. As interpretations they are
  unsupported; as house rules they are workable.

**Verdict: the proposal is not a clarification of the original wording.** The best reading is that the existing test,
*deliberate or not*, decides, with no regard to how often the trap has been used. If you still want the warning
idea, record it openly as a **house rule**, not as a reading of the text.

### 4.5 If the house rule is wanted

Suggested wording, kept as small as possible:

> There is no advantage when the party has fallen down a trap, unless it has done so deliberately. A party that has
> already entered the chamber below, and then withdraws, may later return to it by a trap deliberately and gain the
> advantage. *(House rule: the first party ever to fall down a given trap never gains the advantage, because the fall
> alerts the strangers below.)*

The bracketed sentence is the only change from the original, and it should be flagged as such.

## 5. Corners the wording does not settle

| # | Corner | Suggested answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does knowingly walking into a trap chamber count as "deliberate"? | Yes. The party chose to enter and could have gone elsewhere. |
| 2 | Whose earlier use counts, if "first use" matters? | Any party's, to match the doorway test. |
| 3 | Two traps in one chamber: the dwarf cannot ignore them. | Not a choice, so not deliberate. |
| 4 | Dwarf killed in a fight in the chamber; the party falls *"as soon as it tries to leave"*. | Accidental. No surprise. |
| 5 | The Crypt trap *"has effect once only"* and is *unavoidable*. | Never deliberate, so no surprise. Unaffected. |
| 6 | Surprise needs *"attacking immediately"* and lasts one round. | A deliberately falling party that delays loses it, as with doorways. |
| 7 | A newly discovered chamber below, where the cards are drawn after arrival. | Surprise applies to whatever is drawn; nothing special about traps. |

## 6. Summary

| | For | Against | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** Deliberate falls for dwarf parties | else the dwarf party is worse off than any other; "may ignore" is permissive; nothing forbids it | "ignore" means disregard; the dwarf only guides past; the Chasm shows how optional descent is worded | **Allow it** |
| **Q2** First fall none, later falls surprise | coherent warning story; "another way" principle; re-entry after withdrawal is natural | reverses the "not used before" test; overrides the deliberateness test; contradicts the text twice; trap "continues in effect" | **Not a reading of the text.** The re-entry case is already covered. Keep it only as a labelled house rule. |

**Confidence.** Q1 is a reasonably clear call. Q2 is a closer call, but the text-based case against the proposal is strong, and the part of it that matters in play (a party returning on purpose by a
trap) is already allowed. If the intent is to forbid surprise on a first deliberate fall, that is a design choice for
Peter, and it should be presented to him as a proposed change.

## Addendum: code changes needed to match the recommended interpretations

*Added 09-OCT-2026 after reading the engine (`packages/engine/src`). Nothing has been changed yet. The multiplayer path
and the web UI were not examined beyond a text search.*

1. **Dwarf choice at a trap.** `hazards.ts:200-204` gives a dwarf party `trapAvoided` every time, with no choice. Add a way
   for it to fall instead: either a new action like `descendChasm` (`selectors.ts:183`) or a pause at the trap. The
   party must be able to choose this when re-entering a chamber that already holds a trap, as well as when the trap is
   drawn. With two traps in the chamber the dwarf cannot ignore them, so the fall is not a choice.
2. **Record a deliberate fall.** Add an optional flag beside `fellThroughTrap` (`state.ts:162`), set in `relocateDown`
   (`reduce.ts:540`) and cleared on normal moves (`reduce.ts:674`). It is true for a dwarf party that chose to fall, and
   for any party that knowingly entered a chamber whose trap was already in place. It is false for a trap drawn on that
   entry. Use `delete`, not `= false`, to keep states byte-identical (the SC-EXT-1 convention).
3. **Surprise after a deliberate fall.** `reduce.ts:333` sets `surpriseReady = freshEntry && !fellThroughTrap`. Change it so
   a deliberate fall can gain surprise. `freshEntry` is `!area.visited` (`reduce.ts:449`), which would block the
   return-to-a-visited-chamber case, so a deliberate trap arrival must count as a new route even when the chamber has
   been visited. Accidental falls still gain none.
4. **Retreated-from chambers (needs a ruling).** The `hostileAreas` branch (`reduce.ts:328`) starts the fight with
   surprise to the strangers before item 3 is reached. A party that retreated from a chamber and returns by trap would
   never get its surprise.
5. **No first-use rule.** Nothing to build. The trap keeps "continues in effect", with no "first fall" or "warning spent"
   state.
6. **Unchanged.** Withdraw and retreat stay blocked after a fall (`selectors.ts:169,200`). The Crypt trap stays
   unavoidable and never deliberate.
7. **Rule gaps with no code found.** The "two traps in one chamber" limit, and "dwarf killed in a fight, so the party
   falls on trying to leave". Both are in the rules text but were not part of the debate. Whether a chamber can hold
   duplicate trap cards was not checked.
8. **Multiplayer and UI.** Check that the multiplayer party state carries the new flag (`fellThroughTrap` is already used
   in `multi-zombies.ts`). Add the new action to the UI and the game log mnemonics. Another party must be able to see
   the trap (the visibility point in the question).
9. **Tests.** Update the tests that assert a trap fall never grants surprise. Add tests for a dwarf's deliberate fall,
   a knowing re-entry and an accidental fall. Existing golden and replay tests may change, because logged knowing
   re-entries would now gain surprise.
10. **Spec and rules, per `CLAUDE.md`.** Amend SC-4-16, SC-7.1-11, SC-7.2-9, SC-8.1-1 and SC-9.2-2 in
    `docs/specs/engine-spec.md`, and SC-4-14 only if the fall behaviour changes. Update the Part II prose and Appendix C.
    Add the clarification to the Expanded Consolidated Rules.

Items 1 to 3 are the real changes. Item 4 needs a ruling before anyone builds it.
