UI Review - Summary and Mockup

Task 1 of the UI review work package · 2026-10-30 · source: requirements.md and Peter's Revised UI Presentation 20261029.pdf. A review document: nothing here changes the game.

The proposed interface keeps the 3D play surface clear by default and moves everything else into three on-demand panels: a left slot (party, inventory, or in a fight the combat list), a right slot (card panel, settings, or the test panel) and a central combat dialog. The player is the party leader: every choice, from who fights to who carries what, is made from a selected card. Try it below.

2. Decisions this mockup carries

From the answers

  • The combat list on the left replaces the fan hand; revised fights, solitaire only. Party and inventory panels are not shown in combat: any member, stranger or artifact is selected from the combat list or dialog and shown in the card view.
  • Passive artifacts keep working automatically. Active ones are used from their own card; no "Use X on Y" suggestions anywhere.
  • Settings and Card Panel are mutually exclusive. Remaining system controls (save, log, scoreboard, exit) live in Settings. Chat is out of scope.
  • Blocking popups only where an action must be taken (the trap). Everything else goes through the card view.

Assumptions approved in review

  • Only FightSurface.tsx and its flag are removed. The PvP surface and FightHand stay until the multiplayer follow-up.
  • Treasure pickup and redistribution happen from the card view (replaces the pickup screen and the trade modal).
  • Trap, game over and rule-critical choices (the Sorcerer's terms) are blocking. Dice and outcome notices stay non-blocking.

Interpretations to confirm

  • The T icon opens the test panel (test mode only). We assume it shares the right-hand slot, exclusive with the card panel and Settings.
  • Colour states: gray carrier cannot use it (or it has no use); yellow carried by a member who can use it, but not usable now (this includes wielded passive items); green usable now.
  • The three bottom icons are shown as-is ("remain the same"). The Book icon displays the card panel (Peter: "force it to pop up, on top if necessary, or hide it away if it is hanging around unnecessarily"). Opened from the Book it floats on top of the combat dialog; opened by selecting a card it sits beside the dialog, which narrows to make room.

4. How Peter's mockup reads

Frame

A slim bar with party and inventory icons on the left, the title in the middle and Book (card panel), Settings and T (test panel, test mode only) icons on the right. Panels are dark, translucent and brass-edged, roughly 12-15% of the width each, leaving the board centred and unobstructed.

Lists and cards

List rows are three lines (name, weight, what is wielded) with a letter badge. Selecting a row opens the card panel on the right: the card, then mini-cards of what is carried, then a few lines of manual text, then action buttons.

Combat

The list becomes "NOT ENGAGED" with compact widgets (badge, name, magic chip, strength, total). A central dialog shows one row per pairing. Members are dragged to the Frontline box, then "Deploy Frontline"; the strangers' choices appear and "Roll the Round" finishes.

From the engine's useArtifact actions (selectors.ts) and the treasure table (data/treasures.ts). "Active" artifacts need a player action and get a Use button on their card.

The staff's wake-from-stone use and the Chest's opening sit between categories. They need a rules review before the classification is final, as the answers anticipated. The colour states themselves will be iterated while the inventory panel is built.

7. Open items and future requirements

  • Colour states: iterate on the exact meaning of gray, yellow and green with real play.
  • Test icon location: not a concern to Peter; the mockup keeps it in the right-hand group.
  • Area descriptions: needs the game to remember what the party did at each area (withdrew unseen, approached and found them indifferent); the narrative text is optional behind a verbose-descriptions setting.
  • Tutorial mode and portrait layout: future reviews. This review is landscape only.
  • Multiplayer and PvP: the combat list is built with the intent of replacing the multiplayer UI later; chat is excluded.
  • Structured manual: data model, generator for the HTML export, and rewording the manual-sync rule in CLAUDE.md.
  • Sorcerer's terms: its own task (blocking, multi-step).
  • Accessibility: second cue for colour, keyboard paths for selecting and placing, and a minimum text size.

Suggested next task: build the shell (title bar, left and right slots, panel state machine) behind a flag, then the card panel, then inventory and combat in that order.
