# Sorcerer's Cave Inspired Short Story

Peter mentioned that whenever he is deciding about nuanced rules that he likes to create a scenario in his mind
that explains what might happen. Based on the recent Unicorn rule clarifications for example: 

*I rationalise it to myself with a mini-story for the "no stranger woman" case as follows:

The party in the doorway (including a woman) observes a group of strangers who are in the process of trying
(unsuccessfully) to tame a Unicorn. If the party enters, and subsequently either win a fight, or recruits the
strangers, they are then surprised to find that the Unicorn has changed it's demeanor completely,
and joins the party willingly...'*

I'd like you to examine the overnight game runs and pick an interesting, interaction rich (either with special areas
overcome, stranger encounters resulting in befriending or epic battles, or use of artifacts), medium length game 
that results in the party successfully exiting the cave and gaining a score. 

Having picked a game run I'd like you to create a short story that tells an imaginary story about the game in an
interesting, engaging way using imagination to enrich the interesting highlights of the game run. 

You should plan the story out using best practices for story telling, and use any formulas that an author might use
or a story structure to aid in creating an engaging story.

Having written the plan, then write a short story that should be around two to three pages long - or whatever the 
conventional length would be for a short story. Pretend that you are a published author like Tolkien when doing this,
you can draw on Tolkien mythology if required to provide depth and a back story to the encounter.

Use your best possible model to do this. Having written the story, the next step is to assume the role of an editor,
reviewing the work and modifying the text - as an editor would do - to improve the story.

The story should be written in markdown format and stored along with the plan in the requirements directory.
