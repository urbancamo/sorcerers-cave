**1. The attacker role.**

Swapping roles each round.

**2. Persistence of matches.** Agree

**3. Casualties when the strangers lose.**

Again, my mistake. I failed to spot that inverting the required rolls would upset the Ring bonus mechanism. Strangers
must roll high to get their choice, else strangers with the ring would be penalised. I initially thought that giving the
selection to the human player would help, but it doesn't it just opens up the possibility of an exploit, where the human
chooses the *weakest* to die, knowing that with a ring bonus, 4,5,6,or 7 will select the other creature. Not good. For
now it is better to roll normally, add any ring bonus, and have 4,5,6,7 select the weaker (by strength) creature to die,
choosing randomly to resolve a tie. I hope in the future that a decision required on stranger casualties could be
covered in a **Fight Plan**.

**4. Defender minimum deployment.**

In the reformulated fighting rules 2026-10-05 **[Step c] Defender Prepares Their Defense,** we have the (much clearer,
and compatible with PvP) “*The defender must attempt to deploy at least as many creatures as the attacker has unengaged
creatures.*” This means (for example) a defender who outnumbers their attackers can keep fighters out of the fight if he
wishes, and/or magic users. It is only necessary to strive to meet all potential attacker frontline creatures at least
one-to-one. This would allow a defender party outnumbering the attacker to (for example) keep a Dwarf ring-bearer out of
the fight for the first round – but the Ring still affects all die rolls.

**7. The stranger loadout.**

No, not what was intended. Artefact Loadout should only happen before the first round of a fight. Artefacts dropped by
the dead during the fight stay on the floor. (The rationale is that it is suicidal to stop fighting in order to try to
pick something up from the floor.)

**8. Stranger Fight Preparations.**

The intention was that a Fight Plan would eventually be able to deliver to the stranger party a decision for every
possible stage in a fight (or preparing to fight) where a human player would be required to make a decision. So, when
the Fight Plan procedures are fully specified, a stranger party can call upon the Fight Plan for:

- A loadout plan for artefacts.
- A deployment decision.
- Nominating a stranger casualty.

How strangers allocate themselves.
Point for debate. With latest information supplied, I currently favour Monte Carlo simulation with a cutoff such that
the human player does not notice any delay, and the game is “realistic” in that the longer the human player takes to
decide their tactics, the longer the stranger have to decide theirs.
